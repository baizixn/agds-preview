# Domain learning for game design

Use only through the framed Design Game task or the eligible competitor-research route. Apply the existing [shared task-learning method](../../studio-orchestrator/references/task-learning.md); these are design-specific checks, not another learning workflow, permission, report or evidence grade. Reuse content already inspected in this task. Do not add fields to the Experience Contract validator input or treat its structural result as validation of learning.

## Source judgment and comparison

- Anchor collection to the current design decision and material unknown. Start broad enough to locate materially different approaches, their contexts and important failures; deepen only the mechanisms or production methods that can change that decision. Seek credible adverse evidence as well as supporting examples. Do not stop after the first plausible account or only one or two convenient sources. Coverage and independent evidence, not a universal source quota, determine sufficiency; scarce or inaccessible evidence remains an explicit limit.
- For each decision-bearing claim, separate the author's relevant direct responsibility, the inspectable process or result, and its applicability here. Trace reposts and derivative accounts to their common origin: repetition is not independent corroboration. Official claims, practitioner interpretations and observed player behavior support different conclusions. Reputation, popularity, an official label or a number of agreeing links cannot settle correctness. Record relevant incentives, selection limits and context/version differences where they change confidence; do not invent bias or numeric credibility scores.
- State exactly what was accessed (full text, selected section, process artifact, frames, transcript or excerpt). A search snippet, course outline or unavailable video cannot supply unobserved process, outcomes or causal evidence. A lead can guide another lookup without becoming evidence for a design decision.
- Compare how plausible methods address the same problem: rule and information, player choice, cost/risk, resulting behavior, feedback; or, for production practice, decomposition, authoring steps, iteration and content-cost drivers. Explain assumptions, sacrificed benefits, failure conditions and nearest competing explanation. Inspect coupled mechanisms together when isolating one would destroy the behavior being tested; do not infer a single-factor cause from an inseparable bundle. Keep technical feasibility and production estimates with their owners.
- Resolve disagreement through differences in players, platform, stage, goals and evidence strength, not majority voting. Seek counterexamples to the currently favored explanation. If differences do not resolve the conflict, preserve alternatives and specify the observation needed to distinguish them; research does not itself prove player impact.

## Collection, stopping and design use

Keep the decision question and authorized scope bounded, not an immutable initial list of sources. Add or replace a source when a newly discovered alternative, dependence, contradiction or missing process could change the decision; briefly preserve that reason and rejected/contrary evidence. Do not expand the question silently or continue searching only to support a preferred answer. Systematic comparison of products still requires the explicit competitor route; this reference is not a bypass.

Stop collection when relevant alternatives and credible challenges have been considered, critical claims have inspectable support and transfer limits, and further sources are unlikely to change the bounded decision. If the remaining discriminator requires a prototype, player observation or another specialist, identify it. Reaching a time/access limit is not learning completion: narrow or defer the affected recommendation, state the unresolved uncertainty, and keep unaffected work moving. Do not claim exhaustive genre knowledge.

In the existing Candidate, keep only the useful sources and accessed portions, the comparison that matters, what to reuse/adapt/reject (or retain unchanged), why it fits, unresolved evidence and the smallest authorized observation that could overturn it. Reuse the ordinary hypothesis/falsifier/Adjust/Stop rules. Do not force a design change to demonstrate learning, prescribe a commercial success formula, execute production without authorization, or promote another project's facts into shared Studio knowledge. Detailed provenance may support the existing handoff without becoming a mandatory new report.

Before proposing an observation, name the decision it would inform and the uncertainty it would distinguish. State the actual comparison conditions and what is held fixed; distinguish supplied conditions from your proposed choices. If the brief leaves the comparator open, offer justified alternatives with the different questions they answer, or state a provisional choice and its basis. Do not silently substitute a different baseline, causal claim or required evidence because of source order or wording. New relevant evidence can justify changing the question or comparison: make that reason and the changed implication explicit, within the existing authority boundary. This does not require a unique design, an exhaustive option list or a new report; multiple designs, measures and indivisible mechanism bundles remain available when their decision and evidence limits are clear.
