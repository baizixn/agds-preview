# Experience routing and user visibility

## Cross-owner Experience Trace routing

Producer owns the compact ledger's coverage, scope state, and decision timing. Orchestrator owns routing. The responsible specialists own the content and evidence in their columns:

- Game Design: player promise and mechanic or interaction.
- Art, Audio, or UI responsibility: professional presentation needed for the promise.
- Engineering: observable state, event, interface, lifecycle, or measurement.
- QA: validation method, evidence state, invalidation, and the boundary between automated, expert, device, and human evidence.

Every new feature must point to an approved promise or remain Deferred. Orchestrator may identify missing owners or fields; it must not fabricate their conclusions.

## Experience-quality critique loop

When an approved Experience Quality Contract or stage plan names a material review checkpoint, Producer freezes the decision snapshot and reports the sparse coverage obligations. Orchestrator routes each obligation to the recorded professional owner with the exact contract, claim, artifact/revision, gameplay or use context, evidence cutoff, output boundary, and stable unresolved-gap identity.

Art, Audio, Design, Engineering, QA, UI, accessibility, or another approved owner returns only its own contribution and evidence boundary. Orchestrator preserves `Not Run`, `Inconclusive`, `Supports`, `Falsifies`, stale evidence, and disagreement without voting, averaging, rewriting, or selecting a quality bar. Game Design may integrate the routed states into an Experience Quality Contract assessment; Producer records coverage, freshness, scope, cadence, and gate timing. Direction, target player, quality floor, scope, waiver, and final taste remain Owner decisions.

A material critique return must bind the approved promise and contract, exact artifact/context, evidence and limits, player impact, gap, provisional cause, smallest discriminating experiment, falsifier, responsible owner, and recheck trigger. If that binding is absent, route it as an unverified preference or missing-input question, not as a product revision.

## User Visibility Contract

Before long-running work or any action that controls a visible application surface, record:

- execution visibility: `isolated_background`, `visible_noninteractive`, or `visible_interactive`;
- user participation: `not_required`, `checkpoint_required`, or `continuous_control_required`;
- next observable return: screenshot, playable/checkable artifact, concise state checkpoint, or another task-appropriate result;
- expected checkpoint condition or time basis, without inventing an ETA;
- exact time and token telemetry when exposed by the runtime, otherwise `unknown`;
- which user applications, browsing state, files, processes, or devices remain outside the automation boundary.

This contract explains how the user can observe progress; it does not authorize new external actions or replace tool-specific safety and QA isolation rules.

## Bounded human or native evidence checkpoint

Use this protocol only after QA routes a named evidence gap or the approved claim already requires native-device, expert, human, or player evidence. `checkpoint_required` is a participation request, not proof or acceptance authority.

Record checkpoint identity; Experience Contract and claim; exact artifact/build, configuration, environment and participant role; privacy-minimized stable participant binding and qualification authority; consent or participation state; bounded steps, attempts or exit condition; requested raw observation; evidence limits; prohibited conclusions; QA owner; Reviewer gate; and final Owner authority. Represent the participant only as an opaque token matching `participant-` plus exactly 32 lowercase hexadecimal characters. Reject names, email addresses, free text, delimiters, and differently shaped identifiers without echoing their values. Do not place personal details in reusable Studio contracts.

Completion, refusal, unavailability, identity/target/environment mismatch, or the declared exit condition ends the checkpoint. QA records valid observations or leaves the claim `Inconclusive`/`Not Run`; Orchestrator does not fill the result, retry indefinitely, or convert participation into approval. The unresolved gap may enter Reviewer review with its risk visible. Automation, expert critique, and human/player evidence remain separate and non-substitutable.
