# Task learning

Use this method inside the responsible Skill and the authorized task when the user has pointed out that an existing result misses the requested bar, or an actual check shows a key problem remains, and there is no applicable method already verified for that specific problem. The trigger is the observed result and missing verified remedy, not the worker's self-assessment of skill or familiarity; knowing how to operate a tool is not itself a verified remedy. Name the concrete shortfall, inspect reliable relevant practice, make the smallest authorized attempt, and check its result against the original problem. If it does not improve, do not repeat small adjustments to the same method; compare another plausible method or report the blocker within the existing correction budget. Familiar small repairs with an applicable verified method proceed directly. Learning does not supply missing task framing or widen scope, ownership, tools, permissions, production entries, evidence authority or approval.

## Resolve the gap and try the decision

1. **Start with applicable experience.** Check the current source, existing task evidence and relevant verified practice already available. Reuse it when its conditions still match; identify material differences in version, purpose, scale, inputs, environment and constraints. Do not research again merely to collect links or make the task look thorough.
2. **Research when that basis is insufficient.** Actively seek relevant experienced practitioners' methods and first-hand process: how they framed the problem, made choices, performed the work, checked outputs and handled failure. Prefer inspectable original artifacts, documented process, demonstrations, technical sources or postmortems over promotional summaries. Inspect the portions that answer the gap rather than treating a title, search excerpt, single link or generic tutorial as completed learning. Compare materially different plausible methods and their assumptions, costs and failure conditions; multiple sources repeating one method are not alternatives. There is no source-count quota. Stop gathering when the important uncertainty supports a bounded decision and useful trial, not when a link count is reached. If access or evidence remains insufficient, state the limit and narrow the claim or trial instead of pretending the gap is resolved.
3. **Separate credibility, evidence and transfer.** A person's experience or reputation is a reason to inspect their work, not proof that the content is correct. State what the content actually demonstrates, including omissions, failures and the part you inspected. State separately why it may transfer to this task and what differs. Record source links or identifiers and relevant sections or time ranges when available; say if only text, selected frames or an excerpt was accessible. Do not claim to have watched a process, inspected a source artifact or reproduced a result that you did not access. Treat inferred steps and untested transfer as hypotheses. Preserve provenance, rights and protected-expression boundaries from the responsible Skill.
4. **Make a task decision.** Translate the useful principle into a choice under the current brief, tools, versions, time and authorized constraints. Explain briefly what to reuse, adapt or reject and why the materially different option is less suitable here. Do not import a tutorial's entire workflow or mistake expertise elsewhere for permission or capability here. Keep professional judgments with their existing owners.
5. **Try small, then inspect the product.** Choose the smallest authorized trial that can distinguish the important assumption or failure condition. State the expected observation and what would make the choice fail before execution, then inspect the actual resulting artifact or behavior using the responsible Skill's checks. Reading, a planned probe, a successful command or an attractive example is not validation of the current output. Preserve failed observations and remaining evidence limits; adjust within the task's existing correction budget or report its concrete blocker. Do not introduce a separate retry loop, lower acceptance after seeing results, or infer a higher evidence level from a lower one.

Keep only the useful source, decision, trial result and remaining limitation in the task's existing record or handoff. This method adds no separate report, role, approval stage, ledger, learning library or system. A successful task-level trial remains evidence for its actual scope; shared Skill changes and adoption still follow the existing capability-evolution authority.
