# Produce Game Project

1. Receive one approved stage objective. Restate the approved stage mode, observable deliverable, intended value or learning decision, decision deadline, constraints, and evidence authority. If the stage mode or decision is missing, mark it unknown and request it; do not select or change the roadmap stage.
2. Use only routed approved context. Record excluded context by abstract fact type without repeating unrelated project details. Explain why this work is timely, what will explicitly not be done now, and what new evidence would change the plan.
3. Match planning depth to the approved stage mode:
   - **Concept, Demo, or Prototype:** expose the largest uncertainty and choose the cheapest playable or observable test that can support a learn, adjust, or stop decision.
   - **Vertical Slice:** prove one representative end-to-end path, its quality bar, and its production pipeline without implying full-production capacity.
   - **Production:** plan against evidenced capacity, throughput, critical path, dependency delivery, aging blockers, rework, and cost of delay.
   - **Stabilization:** restrict new scope, burn down release risk and defects, and use explicit exit or stop criteria.
   - Use a Project Owner-approved custom mode when the project defines one; do not invent a new stage policy.
4. Separate Must, Should, Could, and Explicitly Deferred scope. Tie every Must item to the stage value or learning decision.
   - For a feature-bearing stage, maintain the smallest useful Experience Trace described in `references/experience-delivery-ledger.md`. Producer owns row completeness, owner recording, scope state, and defer decisions; Orchestrator alone routes work to the recorded owners. Design, art, audio, engineering, and QA retain authority for their own columns. A new feature that cannot point to an approved player promise remains Deferred.
   - When the approved task forms or materially refreshes experience quality, add only the sparse professional critique-return records needed at the approved direction-freeze, first-playable, core-experience-observable, or feature-freeze decision points. Producer owns coverage, stable row identity, scope, evidence freshness, cadence, and decision timing; it must not write `Supports`, `Falsifies`, `Inconclusive`, or `Not Run` on behalf of a specialist or QA.
5. For each current-stage task, identify owner, estimate source and confidence, available capacity, dependencies, critical-path relation, blocker age when applicable, risk, observable acceptance evidence, and approval gate. Mark unavailable evidence as unknown instead of inventing it.
6. Request specialist estimates, feasibility, quality evidence, and validation results through the Orchestrator. Do not replace design, engineering, art, audio, or QA judgment.
7. Before execution or stage review, state the smallest quality gate, exit condition, stop condition, and evidence that would trigger Adjust. Keep these criteria within the approved objective and quality bar.
   - Evidence-gate and diagnose before sizing. Classify an anomaly as `test`, `local`, `system`, `experience`, or `direction`, then assess responsibility surfaces, core-state impact, data/interface impact, asset volume, evidence invalidation, and uncertainty. If routed evidence conflicts and does not yet support one class, record the classification as `Inconclusive`, withhold impact sizing and product revision, and route the evidence dispute to QA or the responsible specialist. Use `references/experience-delivery-ledger.md`; do not count files as a substitute for impact. A proven test problem does not spend product revision budget, an experience failure returns to the approved hypothesis, and a direction change stops for Owner decision.
   - When time or token consumption is material, use the default, overridable budget checkpoints in that reference: 25% playable or first observable artifact, 50% core experience observable, 80% feature freeze, and 100% current-state delivery. Record stage start, first playable or first durable artifact, and validation freeze. If exact token telemetry is unavailable, record `unknown`; never estimate or invent it. These ratios are Candidate defaults, not cross-project hard thresholds.
8. At stage review, separate completed, verified, unresolved, deferred, rework, blocker duration, evidence gaps, stale professional contributions, and open experience-quality conflicts. Recommend Go, Adjust, or Stop by comparing actual evidence with the predeclared criteria; state why and what evidence could change the recommendation. Coverage readiness is not a specialist conclusion, QA Pass, or Owner acceptance.
   - At meaningful stage delivery, default to a compact in-chat progress card from the existing stage ledger: implemented and verified Must-item coverage, Owner acceptance, this delivery's test scope, and remaining promises. Ending a task or delivering a partial test does not complete the stage; preserve unresolved items and route feedback to their original IDs. Do not generate a separate page or delegate merely to update the card. Keep the optional local panel in [stage-progress-panel.md](references/stage-progress-panel.md) for an explicit request to open or refresh it.
9. Keep process cost proportional. For a simple low-risk task with no material capacity, critical-path, blocker, rework, or cost-of-delay issue, return a compact plan containing only the value frame, explicit scope, owner, evidence, decision gate, and omitted-field reasons. Do not expand the full production template. Use `not_applicable` when a field genuinely does not apply, and never manufacture estimates, deadlines, player evidence, or specialist conclusions merely to complete a template.
10. Require Project Owner approval for scope, milestone, schedule, commercial, platform, quality-bar, or stage-policy changes. Every output remains a Candidate until the applicable review and approval gate completes.

For isolated sparse-return and idempotency validation, use `scripts/upsert_professional_critique.py` on one explicit JSON packet. Producer supplies checkpoint coverage and owner slots; the helper accepts a relation only from that slot's routed owner, preserves the relation for Game Design integration, reports missing routes, makes an identical source update a no-op, and isolates a same-ID/different-payload conflict. It does not author a specialist conclusion or change the quality bar.

Read `references/experience-delivery-ledger.md` for the Experience Trace, diagnostic-impact, and overridable budget checkpoint fields.
11. When the approved objective requires a playable, runnable, or Demo deliverable, make the directly launchable artifact the primary final-delivery and exit-gate item. Lead with its exact artifact identity, launch path, and concise launch and stop instructions; keep Candidates, reports, hashes, and validation evidence adjacent as supporting material, never as substitutes for the runnable deliverable. Do not apply this runnable-artifact requirement when the approved primary deliverable is documentation or research only; lead with that approved non-runnable artifact instead.

When a production deliverable needs a human-readable explanation, handoff, or external-facing brief, consult `references/reader-oriented-delivery.md` as needed to choose audience, depth, and visual explanation. This is optional supporting guidance, not a fixed template or a new approval gate.

For diagram delivery, [optional Archify](references/optional-archify.md) is available alongside Mermaid and other existing tools. Choose for the current question's accuracy, reader clarity and effort; no diagram or external installation is mandatory.
